# Persona: Lena Trujillo *(fictional)*

> **This persona is entirely fictional.** No real student data was used. The name, story, and
> details are invented to represent a realistic archetype drawn from Ivy Tech's publicly described
> student population.

---

## Who she is

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Lena Trujillo *(fictional)* |
| **Age** | 27 |
| **Program** | Business Administration, online — pursuing an associate's degree while keeping her job |
| **Day job** | Accounts-payable clerk, 7 am – 4 pm, 45-minute commute each way |
| **Home** | One-bedroom apartment; 8-year-old daughter; husband works evenings |
| **Device** | Android phone — the only screen available after her daughter is asleep |
| **Language** | Spanish is her first language; she reads English well but dense academic prose is slower for her |
| **Study window** | 11 pm – 1 am, two or three nights a week — after dishes, homework help, and bedtime stories |

---

## One night in her week

It is 11:14 pm on a Tuesday. Lena sits in the kitchen with the light over the stove on so she does
not wake anyone. She opens the course shell on her phone. This week's reading is a 12-page PDF on
supply-chain risk management. She pinch-zooms the PDF to make the text legible, loses her place,
scrolls back, and 20 minutes in she is still on page 3.

She types a question into the textbook's built-in chatbot. It returns a paragraph that uses three
terms she does not recognize, with no glossary. She screenshots the paragraph and opens a
translator. By 12:30 am she has read half the chapter and has no sense of whether she understands
it well enough for Thursday's quiz. The tutoring center closed at 8 pm. She sets an alarm for
5:45 am and goes to bed uncertain.

---

## Frustrations

1. **One format, one pace.** Course readings are PDFs or long web pages — no audio, no plain-language
   version, no "check your understanding" until the graded quiz.
2. **Tutoring hours do not match her hours.** She studies after 11 pm; every human support resource
   closes by 9 pm.
3. **Dense academic English slows her down.** She understands the ideas but re-reading a sentence
   three times costs minutes she does not have.
4. **No progress signal between modules.** She cannot tell whether she is ready for the quiz until
   she takes it.
5. **Phone-hostile materials.** PDFs require zoom-and-scroll; embedded videos will not load on her
   carrier's throttled plan after 10 pm.

---

## Goals

- Finish this degree. It means a promotion and $8,000 more a year.
- Keep up each week — not fall behind and have to drop.
- Understand the material well enough to feel confident in assessments, not just survive them.
- Find out quickly which parts she missed, so she can go back to the right page, not reread everything.

---

## What success looks like for Lena

> "I open one screen, paste in tonight's reading, and ten minutes later I have the five things I need
> to know, three practice questions I can answer on my phone, and a note telling me which part I got
> wrong and exactly where in the reading to fix it. Then I go to sleep on time."

---

## Our framing in one breath

**Persona:** Lena — online working adult, ESL, phone-only, studies 11 pm–1 am.  
**Pillar:** Academic & Instructional Quality.  
**Statewide strategy:** Ensure Consistent Quality Across Learning Modalities.  
**A-Ivy focus:** Learning and study support — AI supports people, grounded in approved sources,
with a path to a human tutor, no data leaving the device.  
**Primary 2030 metric:** Online course completion rate.  
**How the plan moves that metric:** Lena gets an instant, grounded study summary and practice
questions every night she studies. She identifies gaps before the graded quiz, stays on pace
each week, and does not drop. At scale, more students who would have quietly fallen behind
complete their courses instead.

---

## Metrics

| Type | Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary 2030 outcome** | Online course completion rate | The case-defined metric; moving this is the stated goal |
| **Leading indicator 1** | Study sets created between 9 pm and 6 am | Proxy for Lena actually engaging with material in her real study window |
| **Leading indicator 2** | Practice-question accuracy on first attempt | Predicts quiz readiness; a rising score means the summaries and questions are teaching, not just entertaining |
| **Leading indicator 3** | "Get help from a person" requests submitted | Measures whether the AI is handling what it can and correctly routing harder questions to human tutors — the A-Ivy guardrail in action |

The prototype can record and display all three leading indicators without storing any personally
identifiable information.
